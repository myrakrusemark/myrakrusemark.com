Find A Provider

Rethinking a decade-old provider search from the ground up: browse-first, filter-second, so members reach results instead of dead ends.

RoleUX Team Lead
TeamContent Designers, Researchers, Designers, and Accessibility
FocusComplete redesign of provider search for Medicaid, Medicare, and Commercial members
DurationMulti-phase project with structured weekly cadence

The Challenge

Find a Provider was over a decade old. While the tool had evolved incrementally over time, many design choices and conventions had become outdated. Regulatory and contractual limitations had imposed significant constraints on what could be changed. After years of small improvements, we finally had the opportunity to rethink the Provider Search concept from the ground up.

The legacy version suffered from a critical flaw: users were required to create their entire search, including setting all filters, before seeing any results. This often produced zero results, forcing members to abandon their search and start over with limited ability to adjust their parameters on the results page.

Team Leadership & Process

I headed a cross-functional team of Content Designers, Researchers, and Designers. We established a regular cadence that held through the entire project:

  • Internal UX team meetings, 3 times per week
  • External team collaboration, once per week
  • Regular meetings with Accessibility and Design System stakeholders
  • Meticulous tracking of each requirement and its status

The regular touchpoints with Accessibility and Design System stakeholders were crucial. They kept us aligned and never working too far ahead without critical stakeholder input, which prevented costly rework and kept accessibility at the forefront of design decisions.

Three-Phase Approach

Phase 1: Lift and Shift

The first phase identified which parts of the design could be quickly ported to our proprietary design system, letting the developer team start implementation immediately while design and research continued in parallel. Our researchers gathered discovery assets from other Centene products and competitive analyses, building a bank of ideas for the next phase.

Phase 2: Validation and Exploration

We validated the lift-and-shift designs through user research, made quick tweaks in response to findings, and handed implementation to the developers. With the foundation in progress, designers explored new directions for the more complex portions of the application. This is where we made our most significant discovery.

Phase 3: Implementation and Refinement

The final phase brought the learnings and explorations together into a cohesive, user-centered experience, with regular meeting points with external stakeholders and leadership for final touches and launch approvals.

Key Research Insight

Extensive user interviews uncovered the insight that changed our approach:

Members want to get to the "ocean of data" first, and then dig down to the data or providers that are most important to them.

This was the opposite of how the legacy system worked. The old tool made users build a complete, fully-filtered search before showing anything, which frequently returned zero results, and the results page offered too few controls to adjust course. Members abandoned everything and started over.

Design Solution

We redesigned the search experience around a browse-first, filter-second approach.

Simplified Initial Search

We only ask for the broadest of search terms upfront, such as "Urgent Care" or a specialty. Users immediately reach results, often hundreds of them, and gain confidence that providers exist and are available.

Progressive Filtering

On the results page, members set filters as needed and can reset them without losing their search context, which dramatically reduces frustration and improves search success rates.

Clear Guidance and Recovery

Members want clear, concise direction. We present step-by-step instructions at the moments that need them, particularly on zero-result pages, helping members start a new search or adjust filters. A dead end becomes a guided path forward.

Dedicated Provider Details Page

Earlier versions constrained all provider information to the search result card, forcing decisions on incomplete data. The redesign introduced a Provider Details page with room for credentials, specialties, locations, accepted plans, patient reviews, and more, in a clear, organized format that supports informed decisions.

Impact

  • Reduced zero-result scenarios by letting users start broad and refine incrementally
  • Eliminated the need to abandon and restart searches
  • Provided clear recovery paths when searches need adjustment
  • Expanded provider information through dedicated details pages
  • Modernized a decade-old tool while navigating regulatory and contractual constraints
  • Delivered a fully responsive experience across all devices
